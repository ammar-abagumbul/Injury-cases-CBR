JUDGMENT

_________________________

Plaintiff’s Claim

1. The Plaintiff was injured by the Defendant on 30th January 2001 when he used a hammer to knock on the door of the Plaintiff’s room. The hammer hit the Plaintiff’s right eye. As a result she suffered injuries. The Plaintiff now claims for damages arising out of this incident.

2. Defendant appeared for assessment of damages.

Injury & Disabilities

3. As a result the Plaintiff suffered the following injuries:-

> (1) right peri-orbital bruise;

> (2) the visual acuity of the right eye was light perception;

> (3) hyphaema (blood in the anterior chamber of the eye);

> (4) raised intra-ocular pressure.

4. She now suffers the following disabilities:-

> (1) blurring of vision in the right eye;

> (2) photophobia (undue sensitivity to light), occasional discomfort and
discharge from the right eye;

> (3) the visual acuity was 6/18 (70% of normal);

> (4) overall visual impairment of the right eye: 50%;

> (5) 13% impairment of the whole visual system;

> (6) 12% impairment of the whole person.

Plaintiff’s pre-injury earning

5. The Plaintiff was 58 years of age at the time of the injury earning $6,550 as a cashier at Vinci Park Services HK Ltd. with $327.50 MPF. She was granted sick leave for 2 months and 7 days and was dismissed on 30th March 2003.

6. She received $3,165.00 from Social Welfare Department since 26th August 2003.

PSLA

7. The nearest case similar to the Plaintiff’s present disability is Kwan Shek Sang v. Chan Kam Wah [2003] 2 HKLRD 967 in which the Plaintiff was found to have a metallic foreign body embedded in the left half of the right cornea. Examination revealed a right corneal paraxial scar, and the vision of the right eye was 6/12. The permanent visual impairment of the eye was 15%. Further, it was noted that Plaintiff suffered a corneal opacity in the right eye. The best corrected central vision of that eye was 20/80, the impaired peripheral vision suggested that half of the left visual field was missing, and there was also impaired colour vision. PSLA was fixed at $280,000.

Loss of Earning

8. (i) Sick Leave  
She was paid 4/5 of her salary during her sick leave of 2 months and 7 days.
Her loss during the sick leave period is therefore $(6,550 x 1/5 x 2 7/30
months) = $2,925.00.  
(ii) Loss of earning from 30th March 2003 to date of hearing is $(6,550 x 5
months) + [(6,550 – 3,165) x 12 months] = $32,750 + 40,620 = $73,370.  
  
> The total loss of earning is therefore $(2,925 + 73,370) = $76,295.00.

Medical & Travelling Expenses

9. (i) medical $4,000  
(ii) traveling $1,980  
Total $5,980  
  
Loss of Future Earnings

10. Since she is now 61, although she said she could work until 65 I adopt a multiplier of 2. 

11. The loss of future earning is therefore $[(6,550 x 1.05) – 3,165] x 24 = $89,100.

Future Medical Expenses $13,000

12. Damages for the Plaintiff is therefore assessed at:-

> (i) PSLA $280,000  
> Interests at 2% p.a. $280,000 x 2% x 10/12  
> = $5,600 x 10/12 = $4,667.00  
> (ii) Loss of earnings $76,295  
> (iii) Medical & traveling $5,980  
> Interest on special damages  
> $(76,295 + 5,980) x 4% x 3 7/12 + $3,291 x 3.583 = $11,791.00  
> (iv) Future medical $13,000  
>  
> Total $(280,000 + 4,667 + 76,295 + 5,980 + 11,791 + 89,100 + 13,000) =
$480,833.00

13. Costs to the Plaintiff. Certificate for Counsel.

14. Plaintiff’s own costs to be taxed in accordance with Legal Aid Regulations.
