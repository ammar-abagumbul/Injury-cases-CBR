ASSESSMENT OF DAMAGES

__________________________

1. The Plaintiff was struck by a moter cycle ridden by the Defendant on a pedestrian crossing in Jordan Road at about 9.55 a.m. on the 12th December 1980. Interlocutory judgment was entered against the Defendant on the 3rd September 1982.

2. As a result of the accident the Plaintiff (aged 84 at the date of the accident) was admitted to Queen Elizabeth Hospital. She was found to have a fractured pelvis. Emergency laparatomy was performed the day of the accident and retro-peritoneal and extra-peritoneal haemotoma was found. The Plaintiff was discharged from hospital when she was able to walk on crutches.

3. The Plaintiff gave evidence that when she returned home her hip was very painful and that she couldn't walk. She stated she spent 5 months in bed during which time her son employed an amah to care for her. She stated she had to rely on crutches until the summer of 1982. Sines then she has needed a walking stick. The furtherest she can now walk is less than a block.

4. A report was prepared by Dr Wedderburn. His report shows that the Plaintiff still complains of pain in the right buttock, hip, low back and thigh. He said she had difficulty even climbing onto his couch. He said it appeared that her right leg appeared 2" shorter than the other. However, on examination he discovered this was caused by the socket of the hip having been moved up 1" and by the Plaintiff favouring her right leg by tilting the right side of the pelvis upwards by another 1". She has a loss of 10º of her ability to bend the leg at the hip and a loss of 45º in the movements of internal and external rotation of the leg at the hip. Dr Wedderburn stated that her serious injury greatly reduces her mobility and her lifestyle. He also considered that her expectation of life is reduced by at least a year because of the accident.

General Damages

5. Mr Whitley referred me to two authorities on pain suffering and loss of amenities. The first was Li Leung v Yip Kam Wah HCA 416/1980. In that case Silke J. awarded $30,000. The second was Mak Leung Yan v China Motor Bus Ltd. In that case Lui J. dismissed the claim against the Defendant but went on to discuss quantum. $20,000 were awarded for the pain, suffering and loss of amenities. Although both cases involved similar injuries to those suffered by the Plaintiff they were not as serious as hers. As well she has suffered a loss of expectation of life which was not a factor in the two cases cited. Taking into account the authorities cited, the more serious nature of the Plaintiff's injuries, her loss of her expectation of life and the fall in the value of money I assess damages under this head as $45,000.

Special Damages

(a) Hospital Expenses

> Receipts from Queen Elizabeth Hospital totalling $10,303.20 were produced.
Accordingly I award that sum.

(b) Travelling expenses

> These were expenses incurred by the Plaintiff's family in travelling to
visit the Plaintiff in hospital. Based on her son's evidence I consider that
more use was made of taxis than was necessary. I award $500.

(c) Nourishing food

> The Plaintiff claimed $2,500. This figure is on the high side. Adapting To
Ki v Chan Kit-lam and another HCA 2224/1980 I award $10 a day for 6 months.
i.e. $1,825.00.

(d) Wages incurred in employing an amah

> The amah Madam Fung gave evidence that she was employed for a total of 5
months at $1,100 p.m. Clearly her assistance was needed. I award $5,500.
